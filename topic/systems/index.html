A disk-based structure claims to beat an in-memory tree at two billion nodes

The excerpt claims an AVL tree needs 144 GiB of RAM at two billion nodes, while DiskArray uses eight NVMe drives and 264 MiB of RAM yet is faster. The comparison challenges assumptions about asymptotic complexity and memory-resident data structures.

  • WhatDiskArray is an on-disk sorted-set structure compared with an AVL tree at two billion nodes.
  • HowAt that scale, DiskArray uses 264 MiB of RAM and 8 NVMe drives; the AVL tree needs 144 GiB of RAM. DiskArray is reported faster at everything.
  • Why it mattersThe comparison challenges choosing data structures from Big O alone when building indexes for large datasets.
