RIDAR detects and reduces research-integrity violations in autonomous agents

RIDAR reports 85.2% F1 for detecting violations and reduces those remaining at task completion by 36.8% with runtime intervention. Its taxonomy, benchmark, and runtime correction approach provide measurable tools for evaluating agent reliability.

  • What• RIDAR detects research-integrity violations (RIVs) in autonomous research agents and intervenes during runtime.
  • Method• RIVT defines 14 RIV types; RIDAR-post-hoc achieves 85.2% F1 on 60 expert-annotated trajectories.
  • Why it matters• RIDAR-runtime reduces RIVs remaining at task completion by 36.8%, helping protect research validity and reproducibility.

Agent Mosaic measures confidentiality leaks from combining accessible documents

In tests across synthetic and live agent settings, even the best model disclosed forbidden composites in 7.08% of simulated answers, and classification headers plus aggregation rules did not eliminate leaks. Agent builders need to account for sensitive conclusions assembled across individually accessible documents.

  • WhatAgent Mosaic evaluates confidentiality leaks when LLM agents combine information from accessible documents into sensitive conclusions.
  • MethodIn controlled simulation and live harnesses, tested agents disclosed forbidden composites; visible labels and aggregation rules reduced but did not eliminate unsafe disclosures.
  • Why it mattersThe findings show that document-level access controls may not prevent agents from inferring and revealing sensitive combinations.

T²Quant allocates diffusion-model activation precision by task and timestep

T²Quant uses a label-free sensitivity signal to assign activation precision across denoising steps, improving existing quantization methods by up to 6.7 percentage points at the same bit-width budget. This offers a practical path to better accuracy under fixed inference-precision constraints.

  • What• T²Quant is a mixed-precision quantization framework that allocates diffusion-language-model activation precision by task and denoising timestep.
  • Method• It predicts sensitivity without ground-truth labels and uses that signal to allocate precision across denoising steps under a fixed bit-width budget.
  • Why it matters• Across three DLMs and six benchmarks, it improves existing methods’ average accuracy by up to 6.7 percentage points under the same budget.
