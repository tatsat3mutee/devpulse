How we pick

Hundreds of candidates in.
The best few dozen out.

  1. 01

    Collect

    Every morning we pull a few hundred fresh candidates: the Hacker News front page and best list, Lobsters, fast-rising new GitHub repositories, Hugging Face daily papers and trending models, Hacker News searches for AI and systems topics, release notes from the inference and agent frameworks engineers run in production, and engineering blogs from AI labs, infrastructure teams and writers worth following. Anything already published in the last week is dropped, and so are science and medicine journal links that are not about ML.

  2. 02

    Read

    For each candidate we fetch the linked page and keep an excerpt of its own text, plus the preview image the page itself declares.

  3. 03

    Judge

    A language model gives every candidate two separate marks. Quality, from 1 to 10: would a senior engineer want to read this? Depth, evidence and hard-won production lessons score high; a bare title with nothing behind it cannot score above 5. Relevance, from 0 to 3: how close it is to our beat of agents, inference, ML and system design. Off-beat stories (biology, politics, gadgets, retrocomputing) are dropped however good they are.

  4. 04

    Write

    The model writes a plain headline and one reason to read it, using only the title and excerpt. For the stories that make the cut it adds two or three brief points and, when the article describes a concrete architecture, a small diagram of the components it names. It never chooses links or images, and anything it writes that contains a URL is thrown away.

  5. 05

    Balance

    Up to 50 are published. Nearly half the edition is reserved for AI (agents, inference, models, research) and a quarter for systems and architecture, filled only with stories scoring 7 or more. General security is capped at 6 and launches at 6, so they cannot crowd out the beat. Must-reads are the best AI story, the best story from another desk, and the next best. Near-duplicates from different sources are merged, and on a thin day the edition is shorter rather than padded.

  6. 06

    Illustrate

    Lead stories carry the source's own preview image, downloaded once and re-encoded so your browser never contacts a third party. Stories without one get cover art generated from their kind and topic. The pulse line and topic mix are drawn from the day's scores.
