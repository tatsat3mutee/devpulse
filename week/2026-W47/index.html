The week in AI & systems

The 10 stories that mattered most across 6 editions, picked from 297 kept and 1911 scored. Everything here ran in a daily edition first.

Top of the week

  1. InferencePaperWed, Nov 18

    FastFrames cuts video-generation latency with lighter decoding and cache updates

    FastFrames reports that VAE decoding and KV-cache updates account for over half of end-to-end latency, then replaces them with lighter alternatives; its 1.3B model runs at 36 ms per frame on one B200. The profiling and measured changes offer actionable lessons for latency-sensitive inference pipelines.

  2. AgentsPaperThu, Nov 19

    Agent evolution stalls when evaluations cannot reliably identify improvements

    Across seven evolution methods, six models, and more than 10 million LLM calls, the study finds artifact selection no better than random and reported method margins smaller than run-to-run variation. It makes evaluation reliability a central concern for agent optimization.

  3. AgentsPaperFri, Nov 20

    AnyS1 cuts agent cost by compiling routine decisions into one pass

    AnyS1 trains a shared 4B pointer model to handle enumerated workflow decisions and falls back to a larger model when confidence is low; it cuts episode cost by 75–91% across three unseen benchmarks. The results show a concrete way to reduce agent latency and model-call expense without sacrificing task reliability.

  4. AgentsPaperFri, Nov 20

    CompoundingKernel reports 4.40× speedups across 209 GPU-kernel problems

    Its search system archives correct kernels and their lineage, directs parallel agents and checks results outside the model; generated kernels are 4.40× faster than the reference baseline on 209 public problems. The finding that reliable measurement rate—not agent count—limits scaling is useful for designing optimization agents.

  5. Models & trainingReleaseSat, Nov 21

    Inkling 2 Mini uses looped layers and leads sub-100B open models on coding benchmarks

    The 26.4B-parameter model repeats its middle layers three times per token and reports 48.2% on DeepSWE v1.1 and 46.5% on Vals Index v2.2. Its results and laptop-sized footprint make it relevant to engineers choosing models for coding agents and local inference.

  6. Performance & OSBenchmarkSat, Nov 21

    AI-designed Frostbite CPU reports 4.59× performance per watt on 25 workloads

    Frostbite uses an instruction set based on interaction nets and reports 4.59× the performance per watt of a modern dual-issue, in-order core across 25 workloads, including 15 held out for final evaluation. The result makes the co-design approach and its evaluation worth scrutiny by engineers working across hardware and software.

  7. ArchitecturePostmortemTue, Nov 17

    A July gateway defect and reduced capacity triggered Microsoft's email outage

    Microsoft says a July change in network gateway servers failed when load rose and capacity fell, and engineers had to patch the patch rather than roll back. The incident offers hard-won reliability lessons for systems serving users at global scale.

  8. AgentsDeep diveWed, Nov 18

    Claude Code verification loops use tests and graders to check agent output

    The Claude Code team describes end-to-end, property and differential tests, and says a separate rubric-based grader improved task success by up to 10 points in internal structured-generation tests. Reliable verification can let teams delegate more parallel work to coding agents.

  9. Models & trainingPaperSat, Nov 21

    A 27B language model rewrites its weights and gains 6.6 accuracy points

    The authors report that an open 27B model diagnosed its own failures and rewrote its weights without gradients or training, gaining 6.6 points in accuracy. If reproducible, self-rewriting offers a different route to model improvement than retraining or inference-time scaffolding.

  10. Models & trainingPaperThu, Nov 19

    Energy-based recurrent reasoners use far less inference compute on reasoning benchmarks

    Energy-Based Reasoners guide latent-state updates with a learned energy gradient and use exploration-based training; on four algorithmic benchmarks they match or beat prior recurrent models, with 99.9% Sudoku-Extreme accuracy using 345× less inference compute than the next-best model. The method and compute result make this a useful paper for engineers evaluating iterative reasoning models.
