Top of the week

  1. ArchitecturePaperFri, Nov 13

    Evolving Infra cuts agent-serving latency with adaptive compute and KV management

    Evolving Infra separates compute allocation from KV-cache management and adapts both to agent workloads, reducing mean request latency by up to 4.1× and P95 TTFT by up to 14× against Dynamo. It offers concrete serving architecture choices for workloads with changing prefill, decode, and context-reuse patterns.

  2. ArchitectureCase studyWed, Nov 11

    SGLang serves an 816B model on four TPU v7 chips with a fused decode megakernel

    SGLang serves Meta’s 816B Muse Spark Open on four TPU v7 chips, with a 62-layer megakernel that halves output-token time for one or two concurrent requests. The write-up details kernel, KV-cache, and prefill optimizations useful for engineers pushing large models onto constrained accelerator fleets.

  3. ArchitectureCase studyFri, Nov 13

    EU public infrastructure trains a 1T-parameter MoE at 1,300 tokens per second per GPU

    Using PyTorch and TorchTitan, the EUROPA consortium raised throughput from 90 to about 1,300 tokens per second per GPU across 384 GH200 GPUs per data-parallel replica. The scaling result offers concrete lessons for frontier-scale MoE training on shared infrastructure.

  4. SecurityVulnerabilityFri, Nov 13

    PostgreSQL type confusion turns into reliable memory-corruption exploitation

    Changing a field's type after storing a composite value led to a new class of PostgreSQL type-confusion bugs and reliable memory corruption. A flaw in database core is relevant to engineers operating PostgreSQL, including in managed environments.

  5. InferenceBenchmarkMon, Nov 9

    A CPU-only runtime fits a 284B-parameter model in 64 GB of RAM

    The project reports running a 284B-parameter DeepSeek model at 1.71 bits per weight in 64 GB of RAM without a GPU, with 9.1–14.7 tokens/s decode. These measurements make a striking case for CPU-first inference at low memory budgets.

  6. InferenceToolFri, Nov 13

    A project runs 16B models on devices with about 1 GB of RAM

    The project streams weights from flash and uses custom kernels to run 8B and 16B mixture-of-experts models on a TV box and an old iPad; reported speeds were measured on-device. Its memory and kernel techniques are relevant to constrained inference deployments.

  7. Performance & OSDeep diveThu, Nov 12

    Voltage glitching unlocks disabled features on Nvidia’s GA100 GPU

    A voltage fault injection on the GA100 reportedly enabled an 8GB CMP 170HX to expose 80GB of memory, more SMs and disabled features such as NVLink and MIG. The method and results matter to engineers assessing GPU hardware capabilities and security.

  8. Models & trainingDeep diveThu, Nov 12

    Linear programming reproduces a language model’s weight edit with fewer changes

    The author argues that LP solves can reproduce a model weight edit in about a minute, versus eight hours and 28 forward passes, while changing eight times fewer weights. This offers a concrete alternative for engineers exploring weight-space editing.

  9. InferenceBenchmarkTue, Nov 10

    H100 SXM rental prices range from $2.69 to $12.29 per GPU-hour

    The comparison finds a 4.57× spread for H100 SXM 80GB rentals, from RunPod Community to Azure, and separates GPU variants to avoid misleading comparisons. The price gap matters for inference capacity planning and GPU procurement.

  10. ArchitectureCase studyTue, Nov 10

    Amazon replaces LLM ticket labeling with a decision model at 98% lower cost

    Amazon engineers replaced a prompt-heavy Sonnet workflow that assigned tickets to 20 categories with a decision model, reporting equal performance at 98% lower cost. The case shows how bounded classification tasks can avoid generative-model costs at scale.
