LeanGuard blocks forbidden tool calls with Lean-verified policies

LeanGuard checks each tool call against a Lean4 policy and reduces prompt-injection success on DTap from 57.9% to 5.9%, with no forbidden calls observed in the reported Tau-Bench tests. Its fail-closed enforcement and proof-guided repair offer a rigorous approach to agent safeguards.

  • What- LeanGuard checks each proposed tool call against a policy written in Lean4 before it reaches the environment.
  • Method- It fails closed, with decisions proved equivalent to LeanLTL semantics; denied calls can get feedback or proof-guided repair.
  • Why it matters- Verified, fail-closed checks give agent builders a policy boundary; DTap prompt-injection success falls from 57.9% to 5.9%.

Recursa improves domain-task success through recursive self-improvement

Recursa stores skills, instructions and task memories, then uses parallel learners to propose, validate and merge repository improvements; held-out all-pass rates rise from 30% to 55% and 35% to 46% on a legal benchmark. It provides measured evidence for a self-improving agent system without expert supervision.

  • What- Recursa adapts foundation models to new domains by learning from experience without human experts or a stronger teacher model.
  • Method- It bootstraps a knowledge repository from domain materials, then uses parallel learners to synthesize improvements that are validated and merged.
  • Why it matters- On Harvey LAB's Firm Knowledge benchmark, held-out all-pass rates rose from 30.0% to 55.0% with DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and 35.0% to 46.0% with Qwen3.8-Flash.

  • Why it mattersFor large, frequently changing object collections, this data-oriented approach aims to reduce cache misses, fragmentation and fake leaks.
