MACaQ quantizes weights and activations with layer-local MXFP4 transforms

MACaQ applies per-layer activation transforms and Hessian-aware code refinement for W4A4 quantization; it adds 2.3% overhead over pure MXFP4 on Qwen3-32B in vLLM and matches a Llama-3-8B comparison's best perplexity in 11 times less quantization time. The streaming, layer-at-a-time design targets both memory use and practical quantization cost.

  • WhatMACaQ is an MXFP4 W4A4 post-training quantization method for weights and activations that treats each linear layer independently.
  • MethodIt applies per-channel scaling and online block-Hadamard transforms to activations, absorbs inverse transforms into weights, and refines FP4 codes with Hessian-aware search.
  • Why it mattersIts streaming process keeps only one layer in memory at a time and avoids cross-layer dependencies, simplifying quantization for large models.

Tree-Ising edits BF16 weights to repair language-model reasoning loops

Tree-Ising decomposes sparse BF16 weight edits into per-output-row QUBO problems, solved with GPU simulated annealing or simulated bifurcation; it scores 54.6% on MATH-500 versus 51.7% for the agent's edit. The method offers a measured alternative to gradient-based model repair.

  • WhatTree-Ising repairs language-model reasoning loops by casting edits to the model’s BF16 weights as sparse discrete optimization.
  • MethodForward statistics define an objective decomposed into independent QUBO problems per output row, solved on a GPU with simulated annealing or simulated bifurcation.
  • Why it mattersOn MATH-500, Tree-Ising scored 54.6% versus 51.7% for the agent’s edit under the same evaluation protocol.
