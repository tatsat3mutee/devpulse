Agent evolution stalls when evaluations cannot reliably identify improvements

Across seven evolution methods, six models, and more than 10 million LLM calls, the study finds artifact selection no better than random and reported method margins smaller than run-to-run variation. It makes evaluation reliability a central concern for agent optimization.

  • WhatThe Resolution Hypothesis says agent evolution stalls when noisy evaluations cannot reliably distinguish better artifacts from harmful ones.
  • MethodAcross seven methods and six models, selection was no better than random at distinguishing helpful artifacts; method rankings were smaller than run-to-run differences.
  • Why it mattersSelf-improving agents need evaluations that make real gains detectable and accumulable, not just systems that propose candidate improvements.

DWD removes latent decoders and cuts Wan video warping error by 23%

DWD adds a final-step pixel prediction head to latent generators, removing the decoder while preserving the denoising trajectory; on Wan video it reduces warping error by 23%. The method targets both inference cost and visible video artifacts.

  • WhatDecoding Without a Decoder (DWD) lets a finetuned diffusion transformer predict final pixels through a linear head.
  • MethodThe denoising head continues sampling while the DWD head predicts pixels at the final step; on Wan video, warping error fell by 23%.
  • Why it mattersRemoving the decoder can reduce inference cost while preserving or improving pixel quality in image and video generation.
