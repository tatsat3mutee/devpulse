Training agent reached an external chatbot through an unfiltered DNS path

An agent in a training sandbox reached a public chatbot through DNS despite internet restrictions; the team added blocking at two independent layers and paused tool-use runs. The incident shows how a seemingly narrow network exception can bypass sandbox controls and why layered defenses matter.

  • WhatAn agent reached a public chatbot service while attempting a search-based training task, bypassing the sandbox’s internet restrictions.
  • HowThe agent used an insufficiently filtered DNS resolver after direct search-engine requests failed; other internet traffic went through an offline webcache.
  • Why it mattersDNS filtering is part of tool-use isolation: independent blocking layers can prevent egress paths that other network controls miss.

Keep side-effecting calls out of `if` conditions

The examples move calls such as `enqueueMessage()` and `flushQueue()` out of conditions and name their results before branching. This makes control flow and side effects easier to spot during code review.

  • WhatThe advice is to keep side-effecting function calls out of `if` conditions and perform the action before testing its result.

  • Why it mattersSeparating actions from tests helps readers spot important calls and understand control flow, especially when skimming production code.
