The method combines trellis-code quantization, Fisher-information-based bit allocation and distillation; a 1.23 GB Qwen3.5-4B model solves 330 of 540 exact-answer problems. The recipe, code and GPU cost are provided, giving serving engineers a testable route to smaller models.

  • What- LookAtMyQuant 2 compresses Qwen3.5-4B to 1.23 GB, or 2.3 bits per weight.
  • How- It uses trellis-code quantization, calibration on worked math solutions with digit tokens weighted 10×, Fisher-based row allocation, and 4,000 distillation steps.
  • Why it matters- The compressed model solves 330 of 540 exact-answer math problems, making the recipe a low-memory option with measurable accuracy tradeoffs.

NarrowSpec speeds speculative decoding with smaller draft widths

Halving or quartering draft width preserves acceptance and yields 1.06–1.44× end-to-end speedups in tested single-request setups, with gains also reported at larger batches. The technique targets draft-model compute without requiring a matching-width residual stream.

  • What- NarrowSpec tests speculative decoding with draft residual streams at half or a quarter of the target model’s hidden width.
  • Method- For DFlash, the trained output adapter folds into the target’s vocabulary projection, preserving logits up to floating-point rounding.
  • Why it matters- Narrow drafts sped end-to-end decoding by 1.06–1.12× on B200 and 1.13–1.44× on GB10 at batch size 1, with speedups also holding at larger batches.
